Section 02

What this maps to in the CRM today

In your modelIn the CRMWhat we found
Per-partner share % and TDS %✓ Built Every partner already has editable share % and TDS % (from your 3 Aug note that TDS differs by agreement). Defaults sit at 60% / 5% only until a real figure is entered per partner.
Channel pool managed by SBD for the DSA network◐ Different question That pool is not the same field as one partner’s personal share. We should not overwrite the partner default with the pool percentage — they answer different questions. See Question 1.
SBD / RBH / CM as roles that earn overrideNot modelled Staff roles today (National Business Head, Divisional Manager, Business Head–State, Relationship Manager) are access levels. None of them are payees. SBD / RBH / CM would be the first staff roles that get paid through the system.
Override commission on a retained pool, split across a teamNot built The payout engine pays a partner per disbursed file. It has no concept of a staff override — a percentage of a team’s retained revenue, shared across however many people hold that role. That is new arithmetic, not a settings change.
Region org with targets and roll-upNot built States and cities exist (including Madhya Pradesh, Bhopal, Indore). An internal org tree with headcount, monthly targets and region roll-up does not.
State-level monthly P&LNot built Reports today are lead / partner / product / source. A revenue-and-payout P&L per state is what your spreadsheet is doing today; the CRM does not yet.
In plain terms The structure in your model is clear. Most of it has nowhere to live in the CRM yet. Partner share and TDS already do. Staff override, region targets and state P&L would be new work — not a reinterpretation of the existing brief.

Section 03

If this goes into the CRM — what we would build

  • Staff roles for SBD, RBH, CM — access levels like today, plus a mark that they are commission-eligible.
  • An override-payout engine, parallel to the partner one: reads the retained-revenue pool for a period and state, applies your split (editable settings, not hardcoded), and divides RBH / CM pools across however many people hold that role in the region.
  • Region / state org screen — headcount, targets, this month’s volume against target, so a Business Head sees the same picture live instead of only in a spreadsheet.
  • State-level P&L report — channel payout, SBD / RBH / CM totals, company net, against disbursement volume, with optional fixed-overhead lines if you want them.
Scope This is new work. You already said new features get discussed for cost before they get built. We would hold to that here rather than fold it into the Android / iOS milestones.
Section 04

Real questions — only these

We are not asking about the example headcounts, targets or worked rupee figures in the sheet. Those are assumptions you already control. These four are the ones that change how we would build.

1 · Channel pool vs one partner’s share The model’s channel pool is what an SBD manages across the DSA network. Separately, each Sub-DSA / Connector needs a personal share % in the CRM (the field already exists). What should that personal share typically be — a fixed company rate, or set partner-by-partner the way TDS already is?
2 · Do you want this structure inside the CRM at all? Your spreadsheet already calculates the waterfall cleanly. Should the CRM only keep paying partners as it does today, and leave SBD / RBH / CM economics in the sheet — or should staff overrides and state P&L become a live module?
3 · One template for every state, or MP first? The CRM already has seven states loaded. If SBD / RBH / CM is the standard structure everywhere, we would build it once, generically. If MP is a pilot, we keep the first build narrow.
4 · Timing vs the apps Given the ~20 August Partner-app target: should this wait until after the apps ship, or run alongside as its own quoted piece? Either works — we just should not let it quietly extend the store timeline.
What we need from you on the call Answers to 1–4 above. Once those are clear, we can either leave the model as your planning sheet, or quote the staff-override module properly — without mixing it into the app milestones.
