The short answer

No, there is no real payment in it

There is no payment gateway, no bank API, and no money moving through the software. I checked the whole codebase — the only payment-ish word in it is cashfree, and that appears as a KYC verification provider option, never for moving money.

What the payout module actually is

It is a ledger and an approval workflow. It works out what is owed, controls who is allowed to approve it, records that it was paid, and shows the partner. A human still makes the transfer in the bank. That is exactly how nearly every DSA and NBFC in India runs commissions.

Say it like this, and it sounds like a decision — because it is one

“The system calculates the payout, controls who can approve it, and keeps the full record with the UTR. The actual transfer happens in your bank, the way it does today. We deliberately did not put money movement inside the CRM — that needs a payout licence or a bank integration, and it means the software would be holding your funds. Most lenders and DSAs run it exactly this way.”

On the call

Every question he'll ask, answered

“So can we pay partners directly from the system?”
No — and that is deliberate. “The system tells you exactly who to pay and how much, and locks the approval. You make the transfer from your bank, then record the UTR. Putting money movement inside the software means it holds your funds, which needs a payout licence or a bank integration. If you want that later, it's a separate integration we can price.”
Do not apologise for this. It is the normal, correct architecture.
“Can we add automatic payouts later?”
Yes, and here's the honest shape of it. “There are bank payout APIs — RazorpayX, Cashfree Payouts, ICICI or Axis connected banking. It would need a QCred current account approved for that, their KYC, per-transaction charges, and a maker-checker sign-off we already have. It's an integration project, not a setting. Let's do it once the volume justifies it.”
Never say “yes I'll add it” on a call. Say “it's an integration, I'll price it.”
“How does the system know the payout percentage?”
From the lender programme. “Each lender has programmes — Salaried Prime, Self-employed LAP and so on. Each programme carries its own payout percentage and its target bonus slabs. When you book a disbursement you pick the programme, and the commission is calculated from it. You maintain those rates yourself in the Lenders screen.”
“What about the target bonuses?”
Best slab wins, not cumulative. “You set slabs like ‘₹1 crore in the month → extra 0.25%'. The system adds up the month's volume with that lender and applies the highest slab reached. It doesn't stack them, because lender schemes aren't written that way.”
If he says his lenders DO stack them, that's a 20-minute change. Don't argue, note it.
“Who can approve a payout?”
Anyone with the Checker permission — but never the person who raised it. “The system blocks it. Maker raises, Checker approves, and it physically will not let the same login do both. That's the control your lenders and auditors expect.”
“What if the lender hasn't paid us yet?”
That's tracked separately. “There's a step to record that the lender has credited QCred. So you can see payouts you've approved but not yet funded — you decide whether to pay the partner ahead of receipt or wait.”
“Is TDS handled?”
Deducted and shown, but it isn't a tax filing. “The payout shows gross, TDS and net separately, and it's in the export for your CA. The CRM doesn't file returns or generate Form 16A — that stays with your accountant.”
Be clear on this boundary or he'll assume compliance is covered.
