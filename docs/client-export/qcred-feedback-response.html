How the payout module works

You asked to discuss this, so here it is in full — including the part most software is vague about.

First, the important boundary: the CRM does not move money

There is no payment gateway and no bank connection inside the platform. The system calculates what is owed, controls who may approve it, and records that it was paid. The transfer itself is made from your bank, by your team, exactly as it is today.

This is deliberate. Moving money inside the software would mean the software holding your funds, which requires a payout licence or a direct bank integration. Nearly every distribution business in India runs commissions this way — the control comes from the approval trail and the audit record, not from the software touching the money.

The two legs

Money flows in two directions, and the platform tracks both separately. This is the part that prevents the most common dispute in the business — a partner chasing money that the lender has not yet paid you.

Segregation of duties

Whoever raises a payout cannot approve it — the system refuses, it is not a policy someone can forget. A different person with Checker permission approves, and a third step records the payment with its UTR. No single person can take a payout from creation to payment alone.

If you want automated transfers later

It is possible — through a bank payout facility such as RazorpayX, Cashfree Payouts or connected banking with your own bank. It needs a QCred account approved for that service, their onboarding, and carries per-transaction charges. The approval controls already in place are what such an integration would sit behind. Worth revisiting once volumes justify it.

The lender module

You asked for lenders to carry product programmes with payouts and product details, so commissions calculate themselves. That is what this is.

Why programmes, and not one rate per lender

A lender does not pay one commission. The same bank pays differently for a prime salaried case and a self-employed loan against property. So each lender holds as many programmes as they publish, and each programme carries its own payout and its own eligibility.

Lenders that publish grids

Where a lender publishes a grid rather than one number

Thank you for sharing the Poonawalla Fincorp LAP magazine — it changed how we modelled this. Their LTV is not a single figure, it is a grid by property type, and their maximum loan amount changes by city tier:

Poonawalla LAPAs published
LTV by property Residential 80% · Commercial 75% · Industrial 55% · Land/Plot 50% · Specialised collateral 65%
Max loan by tier Metro & Tier 1 ₹25 Cr · Tier 2 ₹7.5 Cr · Tier 3 & 4 ₹3 Cr (Standard Income programme)

Both are optional, and off by default

Plenty of your lenders will quote a single LTV and a single maximum, so the screen asks for one number each. Where a lender does publish a grid, you tick a box and fill only the cells they actually publish — anything left blank falls back to the single figure. A lender without a grid behaves exactly as before.

The Poonawalla Standard Income (EBITDA) programme is already loaded in the system with these exact figures, so you can see how it looks.

Target bonuses

You add slabs such as “₹1 crore in the month, extra 0.25%”. The system totals that month's volume with that lender and applies the highest slab reached — slabs do not stack, since lender schemes are normally written as a single improved rate rather than as accumulating tiers.

Please confirm two things

Do your lenders stack bonus slabs, or does the best one apply? We have built best-slab-wins. If any of your schemes accumulate, tell us before real payouts run.

What are your standard partner share and TDS percentages? The system currently uses 60% and 5% as placeholders. We will set your real figures.

Policy warnings

Where a file sits outside the programme it is being booked under — LTV above the ceiling, FOIR too high, ticket size out of range — the system can flag it rather than silently accepting it. Useful when a junior team member books a case against the wrong programme.
