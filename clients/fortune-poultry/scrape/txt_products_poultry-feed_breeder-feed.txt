[h2] Why Does Hatchability Drop — And Why Is Feed Usually the Cause? 
[p] In breeder farming, every egg has a high financial value. When hatchability drops below 85% — or worse, below 80% — the financial impact is immediate and serious. You are paying to house, feed, and manage birds whose eggs are not producing viable chicks. 
[p] The most common hidden causes of poor hatchability: 
[p] Unsafe feed affecting the reproductive system Aflatoxin does not just suppress general immunity. In breeder birds specifically, it directly affects the reproductive system — reducing fertility in males, affecting embryo development in females, and weakening the immune transfer from hen to chick through the egg. A breeder flock eating unsafe feed produces eggs with lower fertility and weaker embryos that fail during incubation. 
[p] Research shows that 56% of poultry feed in Haryana has unsafe aflatoxin levels — and aflatoxin contamination decreases feed efficiency by up to 10.8%. 
[p] Amino acid imbalance reducing egg fertility Breeder birds have specific amino acid requirements that differ from broiler or layer birds. When lysine, methionine, or other essential amino acids are at incorrect levels, egg fertility drops and early embryo mortality increases. 
[p] Heat stress collapsing production Haryana summers reach 48°C. In open or poorly ventilated facilities, breeder birds under heat stress show egg production drops of 10 to 20%, reduced sperm quality in males, direct reduction in hatch rate, and body condition deterioration affecting the full breeding season. 
[p] Mineral deficiency weakening embryo development Trace minerals — zinc, manganese, selenium, copper — play a critical role in embryo development. A breeder feed without proper trace mineral fortification produces embryos that are weak, develop poorly, and die before hatching. 
[blockquote] Each of these problems is preventable. All of them trace back to feed quality.
