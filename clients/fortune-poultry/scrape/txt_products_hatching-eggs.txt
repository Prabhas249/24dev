[h2] Three Things Determine Whether Your Hatching Eggs Actually Hatch. 
[p] Hatchery buyers and traders who have worked with multiple suppliers know that hatching egg quality is not consistent across sources. The difference between 88% hatch rate and 75% hatch rate from the same incubator is almost always a supply quality issue — not an incubation issue. 
[p] Three factors determine hatching egg quality: ⚠️ 
[p] Factor 1 — Breeder flock health. The egg is only as good as the bird that laid it. A breeder flock under nutritional stress, heat stress, or exposed to unsafe feed produces eggs with lower fertility, weaker embryos, and reduced immune transfer to the chick. The feed the breeder eats directly affects hatch rate. ⚠️ 
[p] Factor 2 — Collection frequency and handling. Every hour an egg sits in a warm nest after laying, embryo quality declines. Collection frequency and the care taken during collection — no cracked shells, clean eggs, correct orientation — directly affects the percentage of eggs that will successfully hatch. ⚠️ 
[p] Factor 3 — Storage conditions. Once collected, hatching eggs must be stored at 15–17°C and 75–85% relative humidity. Temperatures above this range accelerate embryo ageing and reduce viability. Below this range, the embryo can be damaged. Most suppliers cannot maintain these conditions consistently. At Fortune, all three factors are controlled. This is not accidental. It is the result of 14 years of operation and a facility built for consistent performance. 
[p] What happens before the egg reaches your incubator determines everything that follows.
