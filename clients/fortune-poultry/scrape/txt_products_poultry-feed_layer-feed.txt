[h2] Layer Farming Profit Depends on Three Things. Most Farmers Only Control One. 
[p] Layer farming profit comes down to three numbers: egg production percentage, shell quality, and FCR. Most farmers focus on management — how many birds, how often they check nests, how they handle disease. 
[p] But the biggest lever — the one that affects all three numbers at once — is the feed. 
[p] Problem 1 — Thin shells and egg breakage Calcium deficiency is the most common and most expensive hidden problem in layer farming. Thin shells break during collection. They break during transport. The trader rejects them or pays less. You lose revenue on eggs you already produced. 
[p] The cause is almost always the same: incorrect calcium levels in the feed, or calcium that is not being absorbed because Vitamin D3 is missing from the formulation. 
[p] Problem 2 — Dropping production at peak season Layer birds have a laying cycle. If nutrition is not right during the laying phase, production drops before it should — right when market prices are highest. This is often caused by unsafe feed suppressing immunity, or amino acid levels that are not phase-appropriate. 
[p] Problem 3 — High FCR eating into profit Layer FCR determines how much feed you need to produce one egg. The difference between FCR 2.3 and FCR 2.7 across a flock of 10,000 birds over a year is a significant amount of feed — and at current prices, a significant amount of money wasted. 
[blockquote] The farmer who fixes feed quality fixes all three problems at once. 
[p] The calcium and Vitamin D3 combination is the most important. Calcium without D3 is like paying for a product that does not fully work. Fortune includes both — correctly dosed — in every batch.
