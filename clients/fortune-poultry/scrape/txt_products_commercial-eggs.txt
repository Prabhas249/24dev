[h2] Two Problems That Cost Egg Traders Money Every Week. 
[p] Traders who buy commercial eggs in bulk deal with two recurring problems that eat directly into profit: ⚠️ 
[p] Problem 1 — Shell breakage in transit. Broken eggs are lost revenue. You paid for them. You moved them. And they arrive at the buyer as a loss. Shell breakage during transit is almost always a feed problem at the source — layer birds not receiving the right calcium levels produce thin-shelled eggs that cannot survive handling and transport. Calcium alone is not enough. Vitamin D3 must also be present in the feed — without it, the bird cannot absorb and use the calcium it consumes. Many feed formulations include calcium but underdose or skip Vitamin D3. The result is thin shells even when the feed label says calcium is correct. ⚠️ 
[p] Problem 2 — Drug residue rejection. Eggs rejected at the retailer or processor level due to antibiotic or drug residue are a serious and growing problem in the Haryana egg supply chain. Retailers and institutional buyers are increasingly testing for residues. One rejected batch can damage a trader’s relationship with their buyer. The source of drug residues is almost always the same: layer flocks that are not healthy enough to maintain production without antibiotic support. When the feed is right and the environment is managed, birds stay healthy. When the feed is wrong — unsafe, nutritionally imbalanced, low quality — birds get sick and antibiotic use increases. Both problems are feed problems. Fortune fixes both at the source.
