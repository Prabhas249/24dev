[h2] Why Should You Care How Your Feed is Made? 
[p] Most farmers ask “what’s in the feed?” That is a good question. But the better question is: “How consistently can you make the same quality bag — every time, all year round?” 
[p] Anyone can make one good batch. Consistency — the same quality in January and in June, in the first bag and the fiftieth — that requires a documented process. A trained team. And an independent body checking your work. 
[p] That is what ISO 9001:2015 certification means at Fortune. Every step below is part of a system audited and certified by MQA Certification Services — a UK-accredited body. Not our own checklist. An external standard. 🔬 Lab Tested — Every Batch Our Process 
[h2] Step by Step — From Raw Material to Sealed Bag 01

[h3] Grain Storage in 6 Dedicated Godowns 
[p] Accepted grain goes into one of our 6 dedicated godowns — not an open shed, not a shared space. 
[p] Why does this matter? Grain stored in the open picks up moisture. Moisture causes fungal growth. Fungal growth produces the same aflatoxin that is in 56% of Haryana’s feed samples. Our godowns control temperature, moisture, and air circulation — keeping maize in Grade A condition from purchase to milling. 
[p] This is where clean feed actually begins. Not in the lab. In the godown. 03 
[h3] Cleaning and Grading 
[p] Before grinding, raw grain passes through a cleaning process that removes foreign material — stones, dust, husk, anything that should not be there. 
[p] Why this matters: Properly cleaned grain grinds uniformly. Uniform grinding means consistent pellet density. Consistent density means your birds get the same nutrition in every bite — not variable amounts depending on which part of the batch they eat. 04 
[h3] Grinding 
[p] Clean grain is ground to the exact particle size required for each feed phase. 
[p] This is more technical than it sounds. Too coarse — nutrients are poorly absorbed by the bird’s gut. Too fine — feed passes through too quickly and nutrient absorption drops. The right particle size for each phase — Pre-Starter, Starter, Finisher — is specified in our ISO-documented formula. It does not change batch to batch. 05 
[h3] Precision Batching and Mixing 
[p] This is the most critical step in the entire process. Every ingredient is weighed and added in exact proportions according to the formula for that specific feed type and phase.

[h3] High-Temperature Conditioning at 98°C Steam 
[p] The mixed batch goes through a steam conditioning process at 98°C. This single step does three important things: 
[li] Kills harmful bacteria — pathogen elimination before the feed reaches your birds 
[li] Improves starch digestion — gelatinisation makes nutrients more available to the bird’s gut 
[li] Better pellet binding — fewer broken pellets, less feed dust, less waste on your farm floor 
[p] 98°C is a precise number. Not “hot steam.” Not “high heat.” 98°C — documented and verified in our process. 07 
[h3] Pelleting → Cooling → Lab Testing → Packaging 
[p] The final four steps happen in sequence: 
[li] Pelleting — conditioned mix is pressed through a die to form uniform pellets 
[li] Cooling — pellets are cooled to ambient temperature before packaging (hot pellets trap moisture and cause mould) 
[li] Lab Test — this is the final checkpoint. Nutrition value AND safety levels are both tested on the finished batch. If the batch fails — it does not leave the factory. No exceptions. 
[li] Packaging — passed batches go into 50KG or 60KG moisture-resistant bags, sealed and ready for delivery or pickup EC Facility

[h2] How Fortune Keeps Every Batch Safe — Three Layers, Not One 
[p] Most mills do one check, if any. Fortune uses three layers: 01 
[h3] Layer 1 — Raw Material Screening at Intake 
[p] Before any ingredient enters our factory, it is tested. 
[p] Maize — the primary ingredient in all our feeds — is screened for aflatoxin at intake. Any batch of maize that fails does not enter production. This happens before manufacturing even starts. 
[p] Soybean-oil-meal is tested for urease activity. High urease means poor protein digestibility — which means your birds are not getting the protein you are paying for. 
[p] Amino acids are lab-verified for concentration. Underdosed amino acids cause poor muscle growth and reduced egg production. 02 
[h3] Layer 2 — Safety Binders Built Into Every Formulation 
[p] Even after raw material screening, Fortune adds Clinoptilolite-based safety binders into every batch formulation. 
[p] Clinoptilolite is a natural mineral that binds to aflatoxin molecules in the bird’s gut — preventing absorption before it can cause damage. This is a second line of defence, working inside the bird even if trace amounts of aflatoxin pass through screening. 
[p] This ingredient is not optional. It is in every batch, every time. 03 
[h3] Layer 3 — Finished Batch Lab Test Before Dispatch 
[p] After manufacturing is complete — after pelleting, after cooling — the finished batch is tested in our lab before it is loaded for delivery. 
[p] Two parameters are checked on every finished batch: 
[li] Nutrition value — protein percentage, metabolisable energy, amino acid concentration 
[li] Safety levels — aflatoxin within safe limits 
[p] If a batch fails either test, it is not dispatched. Not delayed. Not reworked without re-testing. It does not leave our factory. 
[p] This is Fortune’s policy. No exceptions. Verified Numbers
